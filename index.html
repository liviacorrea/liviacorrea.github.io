Lívia Corrêa McCormack

Postdoctoral Research Associate
Los Alamos National Laboratory

Lívia McCormack is a postdoctoral research associate at Los Alamos National Laboratory, where she investigates polariton physics problems in chiral plasmonic nanostructures and mid-infrared microcavities. She earned her PhD in Mechanical Engineering from the University of Utah in June 2026. Her doctoral research was focused in near-field radiative heat transfer, specifically exploring the role of surface phonon polaritons towards heat transfer enhancement and attenuation. She also holds MSc and BEng degrees in Mechanical Engineering from Rio de Janeiro State University (UERJ), awarded in 2020 and 2018, respectively. During her MSc, analytical and hybrid solutions were developed for heat transfer problems, such as 3D base-2D fins heat sinks, core-annular flow with immiscible liquids, and electronic chips with multiple hot spots.
